# Custom Matching Logic

**Algorithm design.** The matcher is a funnel of four stages, combining deterministic rules with AI, modelled on how human matchmakers and apps such as Shaadi.com and Hinge actually work. **(1) Hard filters, applied in both directions**: we remove only true non-negotiables. These are the dealbreakers stated by *either* person (smoking, drinking, diet, a different religion or caste only when it was marked non-negotiable, a manglik mismatch only when a horoscope match is required, an unwillingness to relocate, a conflict over kids, marital status), plus an age gap more than 2 years outside the stated range. Every exclusion is logged with a readable reason, so the matchmaker can see why a profile wasn't shown. **(2) A weighted, explainable score**: twelve scorers (age, height, income, kids, values and family, lifestyle, location and relocation, background, career parity, marriage timeline, interests, preference fit) each return a score from 0 to 1 using smooth curves rather than hard cutoffs, plus a specific human reason (for example, "Ananya is 3 years younger, within the ideal 2–5 year gap"). The weights depend on gender and are stored as config. For **male clients** we implemented the brief's rules: she is younger (best at 2–5 years), shorter (best at 5–15 cm), earns less (with a gentle decline above his income), and has the same views on kids (Yes against No scores roughly 0). These are weighted *soft* preferences, so a 1 cm difference never hides a great match. For **female clients** the weighting is led by research: career and education parity and family values (18% each), who relocates, including NRI visa status (14%), kids and marriage timeline (14%), and financial stability measured against *her* stated minimum rather than a "must earn more" rule (10%). **(3) Reciprocity**: we also score how well the client fits each candidate, using the candidate's *own* gender profile blended with the candidate's stated partner preferences. The two directions are combined with a **geometric mean** √(forward × reverse), the approach OkCupid uses, so one-sided matches (90/30 → 52) drop down the list, which reflects reality: the other person would say no. A small quality multiplier (±5%, for verified ID, LinkedIn and income, and the marriage timeline) breaks ties. An **MMR diversity re-rank** then makes sure the matchmaker sees variety rather than ten near-identical profiles. Scores are calibrated so that on our data the median pair scores 69 and the top 10% score 86 or more, which feeds four tiers: High Potential (80+), Strong (65+), Worth Exploring (50+) and Low.

**AI integration.** AI is the fourth stage, and it adds what rules cannot: the **matchmaker's unstructured notes** from calls and meetings ("wants someone who reads", "mom prefers a Pune-based family", "had a bad long-distance experience"). For each client's top 10 matches we make **one batched LLM call** (OpenAI). It receives the two profiles with personal details removed, the rule-based sub-scores and reasons, and the notes. It returns structured, schema-validated JSON for each match: a **bounded score adjustment (±10)**, a label, a one-line summary, "why it works", "watch-outs" and a conversation starter. Because the adjustment is capped, the LLM can re-order matches using nuance but cannot overturn the deterministic logic, and every final score remains auditable (mutual score + AI adjustment are shown in the score breakdown). AI also **drafts personalised intro emails** in Warm, Formal or Playful tone. Each email references specific common ground and includes the match's basic profile, and the matchmaker can edit it before sending. A third feature **summarises a client's notes** into a brief with inferred preferences and a suggested next step. Safeguards: email addresses, phone numbers, surnames and exact dates of birth are never sent to the model. Results are cached by a hash of the inputs. Every AI feature has a deterministic fallback (rules plus keyword matching of the notes), so the product works, and the demo never breaks, without an API key.

**Assumptions.** (1) Matching is between men and women, as the brief specifies, but the engine works the same for any gender (weights are keyed by the seeker's profile), so it can be extended. (2) The brief's male-client rules are implemented as heavily weighted preferences rather than filters, and the matchmaker can still see every reason behind a score. (3) Caste, religion, mother tongue and horoscope count only when the client says they matter, reflecting the "caste no bar" trend on modern platforms. Complexion is deliberately not collected. (4) Pool profiles are synthetic but realistic: deterministic seed data with consistent links between community, region, language, diet, education, employer and income, and with plausible partner preferences, which is what makes reciprocal scoring meaningful. (5) Income is compared in lakhs per annum (LPA) without adjusting for cost of living for NRIs. (6) The algorithm advises and the matchmaker decides: profiles already sent are excluded, and outcomes (Accepted, Declined, Met) are recorded, which lays the groundwork for a feedback loop that adjusts each client's weights. That loop is a planned next step and is not built.
